setup

Connect in a minute. No token wrangling.

Cinemetric uses Sign in with Plex, the same approval screen other Plex apps use. You approve it in your browser, pick a server and you're done.

  • Your token goes straight from Plex into a private file on your computer. It never appears in the chat.
  • Lists every server your account can use and tests each address, preferring local, encrypted ones.
  • Optionally adds Tautulli for richer watch stats, without the API key passing through the chat either.
  • Shows up in Plex under Authorized Devices, so you can revoke it any time.

library-report

Know exactly what's on the shelf.

A full picture of every library: how much you have, how much space it takes and what quality it's in, plus the little things that need tidying up.

  • Item counts and storage used, per library and in total.
  • Quality breakdowns: 4K, 1080p and lower, video and audio codecs, and 10-bit video.
  • What was added recently, and how much was added each month: items and storage.
  • Housekeeping: unmatched items, missing posters, unavailable files and very large files.
  • Duplicates: titles with more than one copy, or in more than one library, and the space the extras use.
  • Upgrade candidates: titles only in SD or 720p, skipping any you already have in better quality.
  • Music: how much is lossless or lossy, lossy bitrates, mixed albums, and albums or artists missing artwork.

Ask Claude: Give me a Plex library report, What's been added to Movies recently?, Do I have any duplicate movies?, Which movies are worth upgrading? or How fast is my library growing?

server-health

A checkup for your server, on demand.

See how Plex is doing right now and what deserves a look, from available updates to why someone's stream is buffering.

  • Version and available updates, and whether remote access works.
  • CPU and memory use.
  • Who's streaming right now: direct play or transcode, and how much bandwidth.
  • Streaming settings, like hardware transcoding and your remote streaming limit.
  • Running tasks (and any that look stuck), library scan freshness and scheduled maintenance, with anything off flagged for you.

Ask Claude: How's my Plex server doing? or Why is Plex transcoding?

watch-activity

See what everyone's been watching.

Who's watching right now, plus plays, watch time, favourites and habits over the last 30 days, or any period you ask about.

  • Who's watching right now, on which device, and how far along they are.
  • Total plays and watch time.
  • Most watched movies, shows and music.
  • Most active users and the devices they use.
  • Daily trends and recent plays.
  • The most streams at once, and titles started but never finished.
  • Everyone, or just one person.
  • Uses Tautulli if you have it; otherwise Plex's own history (which counts plays only).

Ask Claude: What's been watched on Plex this month?, Who's watching right now? or What has Sam watched lately?

users-and-shares

Know exactly who can see what.

Everyone your server is shared with, in one place, instead of one person at a time in Plex's settings.

  • Friends, Plex Home members, managed users and pending invites.
  • Which libraries each person can see, and who can see each library.
  • Who can download, who has content restrictions, and when each person last played something.
  • Which shared libraries the people who can see them actually play from.
  • Flags old invites, people who haven't watched in months, shared libraries nobody has played from lately, and "all libraries" shares.
  • Reads the share list from your plex.tv account (read-only). Never shows anyone's email address.

Ask Claude: Who have I shared my Plex server with? or Who can see my Kids library?

unwatched

See where your space goes, and whether anyone uses it.

Movies and shows that have sat for months without anyone finishing them, largest first. Just the facts: it never tells you what to delete.

  • Titles added more than 6 months ago that nobody has finished since (or any number of months you ask for).
  • How much space each one uses, when it was added, and when it was last finished, or never.
  • Totals per library and the share of your storage they take up.
  • Counts plays by everyone on your server, not just you. Only finished plays count.
  • Uses Tautulli if you have it; otherwise Plex's own watch history.

Ask Claude: What on my Plex server has nobody watched in months? or Which shows has nobody finished this year?

what-to-watch

Find something good to watch tonight.

A friendly pick from your own library, not a catalog. Say what you're in the mood for and get a few suggestions, each with a reason.

  • Filter by genre, length, decade, rating or content rating, or just ask for something you haven't seen.
  • Shuffled every time, so asking again gives fresh ideas.
  • Pick up where you left off: what you're partway through and the next episode up.
  • Only titles already on your server, never suggestions from somewhere else.
  • "Haven't seen" means your own account's watch status in Plex.

Ask Claude: Find me a comedy under 2 hours I haven't seen or Pick up where I left off

show-progress

Know where everyone is in every show.

Follow each person through a series, not just single plays: who's caught up, who has new episodes waiting, and which shows people stopped partway through.

  • Each person's place in a show: the furthest episode they finished, how many are left and what's next.
  • Caught up, new episodes waiting, partway through, or stopped (nothing played in 3 months, or any number you choose).
  • Rewatching early episodes or starting at season 3 doesn't count as falling behind.
  • Which shows got new episodes lately, and whether the people following them have watched them.
  • Uses Tautulli if you have it; otherwise Plex's own watch history.

Ask Claude: Where is everyone in our shows? or Which shows did people stop watching partway through?

changes

See what changed since last time.

Each time you ask what changed (or build the dashboard), Cinemetric saves a small snapshot on your computer. The next time, on another day, it tells you what arrived, what went missing and who got access since then.

  • Titles added and removed in each library, and new episodes by show.
  • Files Plex can no longer find, and ones that came back.
  • Plex updates, remote access changes, and people given or losing access.
  • The library, server and sharing reports and the dashboard show the same "since last time" changes.
  • Nothing runs in the background: a snapshot is only saved when you ask. Ask a week apart and you see the whole week.
  • Snapshots stay private on your computer, hold no emails or watch history, and are deleted after 90 days.

Ask Claude: What changed on my Plex this week? or Did anything go missing from Plex?

episode-gaps

Spot the episodes that slipped through.

Finds the holes in your TV shows using only what's on your server: an episode missing between two you have, a season that starts late, a season missing between others.

  • E01, E02 and E04 are there? It points out E03.
  • Seasons that start late, seasons missing in the middle, and episodes whose files Plex can't find.
  • Specials, files holding two episodes and shows numbered straight on across seasons aren't counted as gaps.
  • Honest about its limits: it can't see episodes after the last one you have, because it never asks an online service.
  • Just the facts: it never tells you where to get anything.

Ask Claude: Am I missing any episodes on Plex? or Is anything missing from Lanterns?

playback-check

Find out why Plex keeps transcoding.

Checks every movie and episode for the usual reasons a file gets converted on the way to your TV, and with Tautulli, shows which devices and people it really happens to.

  • Image-based subtitles (PGS, VobSub), which make Plex redraw the whole picture while subtitles are on.
  • TrueHD and DTS audio that many TVs and streaming sticks can't play, and whether a file has a common track to switch to.
  • Files above your server's remote streaming limit, or a speed you choose.
  • With Tautulli: the devices and people transcoded most, how often, and why.
  • Says "likely", not "will", and never tells you to convert your files.

Ask Claude: Why does Plex keep transcoding? or Which movies won't play directly on my Roku?

subtitles-and-languages

Find the films nobody can follow.

Checks the language of every audio and subtitle track, so you find the foreign film with no subtitles you can read before movie night, not during it.

  • Movies and episodes with audio in another language and no subtitles in yours.
  • For TV, which episodes, such as S02E04 to E07, so you can go straight to the files.
  • Titles missing subtitles in a language you name, such as Spanish for family visiting.
  • Tracks with no language set, which stop Plex picking the right one.
  • How many files have audio and subtitles in each language.
  • Forced subtitles, which usually cover only a few lines, aren't counted as full subtitles.

Ask Claude: Which foreign films can't we follow? or Which movies are missing Spanish subtitles?

title-lookup

Everything about one title, in one answer.

Ask about a movie, a show or a single episode and get what Cinemetric knows about it: its files and quality, whether it's likely to transcode, and who watched it.

  • Each version's resolution, HDR, codecs and size, and the libraries it's in.
  • Likely transcode causes for each file, by the same rules as playback-check.
  • For a show: seasons, episode counts, sizes, and episodes whose files Plex can't find.
  • Who watched it, how often, when, and how far they got.
  • Several titles match? Claude asks which one you meant instead of guessing.

Ask Claude: Tell me about Harbor Lights on my Plex or Who's watched Lanterns S02E03?

year-in-review

A year of watching, on one page.

Hours watched, the busiest months and the busiest day, movies vs TV vs music, and the most watched movies, shows and artists for any calendar year.

  • The whole-server recap names no one, and leaves off titles only one person watched, so it never gives away what someone else watches.
  • Or make one of your own year, or one person's to give to them.
  • Saved on your computer, as a private claude.ai page, or both. Run it again in December and the same page updates.
  • Hours come from Tautulli; with Plex alone it counts plays.

Ask Claude: Make our Plex year in review or Show me my year on Plex

dashboard

The big picture, on one good-looking page.

Library stats, server status (version, updates, remote access and anything that needs a look), the last 30 days of watching, what nobody has finished in a while, gaps in your TV episodes, files likely to transcode, and who your server is shared with, together, with charts. Ask Claude to refresh it whenever you like.

  • Save it on your computer, as a private claude.ai page you can open from anywhere, or both.
  • Each refresh updates the same file and the same link, so your bookmarks keep working.
  • Charts for plays per day, storage added per month, and trends over the saved snapshots: storage, titles per library and people with access.
  • Can hide people's names (viewers and the people you share with), handy before you share it.
  • Nothing runs in the background. It updates only when you ask.

Extras for Claude Code

Mods, built right in.

Mods are small add-ons that run inside Claude Code (the terminal and the desktop app's Code tab). They come with Cinemetric, so there's nothing extra to install, and each one has its own on/off switch.

Read-only guard

On by default Name: guard

Cinemetric's skills only read from your server. The guard also checks the commands Claude writes on its own: if one would change something on your Plex server, Tautulli or plex.tv (deleting a title, starting a library scan, marking something watched), it stops it before it runs. It also hides your Plex token and Tautulli API key from what Claude sees, and stops Claude putting them in files, issues or pages.

A safety net, not a guarantee. It reads each command before it runs, so it can't see inside a program Claude saves to a file and runs later. It's meant to be turned off only by you.

Library status line

Off by default Name: status

A quiet line just above the prompt showing how big your library is and when Cinemetric last checked it:

Plex: 1,970 movies · 417 shows · 59.4 TB · checked today

It never contacts your server. Instead it reads the snapshot Cinemetric saves each time you ask what changed or refresh the dashboard, so it's only as current as the last time you did one of those.

Now Playing

Off by default Name: now

Type /cinemetric-now to open a panel showing who's streaming right now: what they're watching, on which device, and whether it's playing directly or being transcoded:

3 streams · 1 transcoding · 31.2 Mbps · updated 9:41 PMalex · Arrival (2016) · Roku · playing 42% · direct playsam · Dune (2021) · iPhone · paused 10% · transcode (video)jo · Bluey S02E14 · iPad · playing 77% · direct stream

It checks your server about every 30 seconds, only while the panel is open, and what it shows stays on your screen, never sent to Claude.

Shows who's watching. Since the panel lists people by name, it never opens by itself, only when you ask.

Switching mods on and off

Type this in Claude Code to see every mod and whether it's on:

/cinemetric-mods

Add a mod's name and on or off to switch it. For example, to turn on the status line:

/cinemetric-mods status on

Each mod also has a switch in Claude Code's settings menu, opened with /config.

The guard is the one exception to who can switch things: it's meant to be turned off only by you. Cinemetric refuses Claude's usual ways of turning it off, so Claude can't casually switch off its own safety net.

Mods need Claude Code 2.1.260 or newer, and work best in your terminal: some versions of the desktop app can't show the status line or the Now Playing panel. If /cinemetric-mods says it isn't installed, every skill still works, and the README explains how to switch the mods on.

Built to be trusted

Look, don't touch.

Cinemetric reads from your server and never writes to it, and the read-only guard, an extra safety net, stops Claude's own commands from changing it and keeps your Plex token and Tautulli API key out of Claude's sight. Your keys stay with you.

  • Read-only, always

    Every skill only reads. Nothing on your Plex server or in Tautulli is ever deleted, renamed or changed.

  • Keys stay private

    Your Plex token and Tautulli key go straight into a file only you can read. They never pass through the chat.

  • Encrypted by default

    Certificates are checked unless you say otherwise. It only talks to your server, Tautulli and plex.tv (for sign-in and your share list).

  • Nothing to install

    Small Python scripts using only the standard library. No pip installs, no background services, no SSH.

Getting started

Up and running in four steps.

You'll need Claude Code 2.1.75 or newer (2.1.260 or newer for mods), Python 3.8 or newer, and a computer that can reach your Plex server over the network. Cinemetric works best in Claude Code in your favorite terminal.

Do each of these steps in Claude Code:

  1. 1

    Add the marketplace

    /plugin marketplace add jeremehancock/cinemetric
  2. 2

    Install the plugin

    /plugin install cinemetric@cinemetric
  3. 3

    Connect your server

    Ask Claude Set up Cinemetric (or run the command below). Open the Sign in with Plex link it gives you, approve Cinemetric, tell Claude you're done, then pick your server. Cinemetric tests the connection and saves it.

    /cinemetric:setup
  4. 4

    Start asking

    • Give me a Plex library report
    • How's my Plex server doing?
    • What's been watched on Plex this month?
    • Build my Plex dashboard
Add Tautulli (optional)

If you run Tautulli, ask Claude to add Tautulli to Cinemetric and tell it your Tautulli address. You get watch time, devices and busiest times. Your API key never goes through the chat:

  • No login on Tautulli? Cinemetric fetches the key from Tautulli itself.
  • Tautulli has a login? Claude gives you a link to a small one-time page that runs only on your computer. Paste the key from Settings → Web Interface → API and click Test and save.
  • Claude Code on another machine (over SSH or in the cloud)? Claude gives you a command to run in a terminal on that machine instead.

Full Tautulli details in the README

Mods (optional)

Mods come with Cinemetric, so there's nothing more to install. The read-only guard is already on. Everything else, like the library status line, starts off. To see them all and whether each is on:

/cinemetric-mods

Mods need Claude Code 2.1.260 or newer. What each mod does and how to switch them

Set up manually

Create ~/.config/cinemetric/config.json yourself, in your own editor (don't paste your token into a chat):

{
  "plex_url": "http://192.168.1.10:32400",
  "plex_token": "YOUR-TOKEN-HERE"
}

Then make it private to you:

chmod 600 ~/.config/cinemetric/config.json

You can also use PLEX_URL and PLEX_TOKEN environment variables instead.

Manual setup in the README

Revoke access

In Plex, go to Settings → Authorized Devices and remove Cinemetric. Run setup again any time to reconnect. If you used manual setup and think a token leaked, use sign out of all devices in your Plex account settings.

AI can make mistakes

Cinemetric's scripts collect the numbers, but the reports you read are written by Claude, an AI model. It can misread data or draw a conclusion the numbers don't fully support. Double-check anything important in Plex or Tautulli before acting on it, especially before deleting, replacing or re-encoding files.

Changes are up to you

Cinemetric only reads, but Claude can do more than run its skills. If you ask Claude to change something on your server, Claude Code shows you each command first, so give it a read before you approve. For extra peace of mind, switch off Allow media deletion in Plex (Settings, Library), and Plex won't delete media files for any app.
